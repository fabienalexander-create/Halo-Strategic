## 1 — Strategic fit check against the Halo Bible

Before accepting the brief's positioning at face value, checked it against `docs/HALO_BIBLE.md` (frozen, constitutional) rather than let a new campaign invent parallel language:

- **"Diagnosis before prescription"** (Halo's First Law) maps directly onto Concept D's "before you redesign, find out what's actually wrong" — this is the one creative concept that states the First Law almost verbatim. Correctly identified in the original brief as the strongest strategic fit.
- **"Websites... are delivery mechanisms, not products"** (`docs/PRODUCT_POSITIONING.md`, governing principle). The brief's own instruction not to sound like a web agency (§15) is already the right instinct and is consistent with this. The copy below leans on Commercial Leakage Framework language ("the path from visitor to enquiry," "friction," "conversion") rather than web-design language ("design," "build," "redesign") wherever it doesn't cost clarity.
- **The six constraint areas** (`docs/HALO_BIBLE.md` Section Four) give this campaign a specific, evidenced vocabulary instead of the generic "conversion, UX, messaging" list in §03 of the original brief. A website review under this campaign is really Acquisition Systems ("which channels produce customers, versus which produce leads that never convert") plus Conversion & Sales Systems ("how long between a lead arriving and a human actually engaging it") applied narrowly to one channel. Worth saying this once, internally, so the review itself doesn't drift into generic web-audit territory.
- **No invented statistics, no invented guarantees, no fake scarcity.** The brief already states this correctly (§13, §28). Nothing below breaks it.

## 2 — Creative Director's assessment of the four concepts

Evaluated against the brief's own eight criteria (§28): scroll-stop, clarity, credibility, Halo brand fit, commercial relevance, lead-gen potential, visual distinctiveness, likelihood of attracting the right lead.

| | Concept A: Problem | Concept B: Cost | Concept C: Diagnosis | Concept D: Prescription Challenge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scroll-stop | Strong, direct question | Strong, slightly heavier | Moderate, softest hook | Strong, pattern-interrupt structure |
| Clarity | Very high | Very high | High | High but needs the two-line structure to land |
| Credibility | High | Medium, "actually costing" risks sounding like a scare tactic | High | Highest, sounds like something only a diagnostic firm would say |
| Halo brand fit | High | Medium | High | Highest, is the First Law |
| Commercial relevance | High | High | Medium, softer framing undersells the commercial angle | Highest |
| Lead-gen potential (volume) | Highest, broadest appeal | High | Highest, least confrontational | Medium, narrower, self-selects for people already sceptical of agencies |
| Right-lead vs. cheap-lead | Good | Risk: "costing your business" language can attract anxious clickers who aren't real buyers | Good | Best: filters for exactly the audience already primed to distrust a redesign pitch |
| Visual distinctiveness | Baseline for the whole system | Same system, marginal difference | Same system, marginal difference | Same system, but two-line copy structure gives it a different rhythm |

**Recommendation: run two concepts, not four.**

The brief's own optimisation section (§22) says not to change anything before 48 hours because "we need to allow enough data to accumulate to identify meaningful patterns" on a £200 budget. That's the right instinct, and it argues against its own §27 test matrix. At £10/day for 20 days, splitting evenly across four creatives gives each one roughly £50 and 5 days, which is too thin a slice to read cleanly against a £40 break-even CPL, let alone to compare four variants against each other with any confidence. Halo's own First Law applies to the campaign's own methodology here: don't run a test you don't have enough evidence to read.

**Hero: Concept A (The Commercial Problem).** Broadest reach, cleanest read, least risk, the right creative to establish whether the UK owner/director + 2-50 employee hypothesis works at all. This is what the initial £200 needs to answer before anything else matters.

**Challenger: Concept D (The Prescription Challenge).** Halo's most distinctive line, worth testing even on a small budget because it's the one creative that could reasonably out-qualify Concept A on lead quality even if it underperforms on raw CPL, which is exactly the "cost per qualified opportunity, not raw CPL" principle the brief already states in §06 and §22.

**Concepts B and C: hold, don't build yet.** B risks the "unsupported accusation" tone the brief itself warns against in §03. C is the softest and least differentiated from generic web-agency copy: "could be working harder" is the one line in this brief that a competitor could publish unchanged. Neither is wrong enough to discard permanently, both are candidates for a second-wave test once A vs. D produces a clean read on what actually works with this audience.

This is a change from the original four-way matrix. Flagging it as a recommendation Fabien should confirm before the creative-production module starts, not a unilateral cut.

## 3 — Finalised copy: Concept A (Hero)

**Headline (creative):**
IS YOUR WEBSITE
LOSING YOU
ENQUIRIES?

**Supporting line:** Find out what's getting in the way.

**Bronze accent / offer line:** FREE WEBSITE REVIEW

**Introductory text (LinkedIn post copy, above the creative):**

> Your website might look good. That's not the same question as whether it's turning visitors into enquiries.
>
> Halo Strategic offers a free website review: a practical look at where your site may be creating friction between a visitor arriving and that visitor actually getting in touch. No obligation, just what we'd actually tell you if you asked.

**Headline (ad unit):** Is Your Website Losing You Enquiries?

**CTA button:** Request Review

Kept close to the brief's Copy Version A, tightened per Editorial Standards (no em dashes, direct sentences, no "we'll identify" list-of-nouns construction that reads like a services menu).

**Headline (creative):**
BEFORE YOU REDESIGN
YOUR WEBSITE

**Line two (bronze):** FIND OUT WHAT'S ACTUALLY WRONG WITH IT.

**Bronze accent / offer line:** FREE WEBSITE REVIEW

**Introductory text (LinkedIn post copy, above the creative):**

> Before you spend on a redesign, ask a more useful question first: what's actually stopping your current website from converting?
>
> We'll look at your website and point out where visitors are most likely dropping off before they ever become an enquiry. No pitch for a new site. Just what we see.

**Headline (ad unit):** Find What's Holding Your Website Back

**CTA button:** Get Free Review

"No pitch for a new site" is added, not in the original brief, because it's the single sentence that makes Concept D's promise credible: it explicitly rules out the thing a web agency would do next, which is exactly the differentiation §15 asks for.

## 5 — Audience

UK-based. Job titles: Founder, Co-Founder, Owner, Business Owner, CEO, Managing Director, Director, President. Company size: 2-50 employees. Single audience for the initial £200 test, per the brief; the broader UK audience is a second-wave test, not run concurrently.

## 6 — Economics

£200 total test spend, £10/day for approximately 20 days. Revenue model: ~£40 per qualified lead, so £40 is break-even CPL, with the qualified-CPL bands from the original brief kept unchanged (£10 exceptional through £50+ rework required). Optimising for cost per qualified lead, not platform-reported CPL, per §05.

With two creatives instead of four (§2 above), each gets roughly £100 and 10 days, still thin against a £40 CPL target, but a meaningfully more readable split than four ways.

## 7 — Lead Gen Form

Fields (4, at LinkedIn's recommended 3-4): First name, Work email, Company name, Company website. Qualifying question: "What would you most like to improve about your website?" with options Generate more enquiries / Improve conversion / Improve design / Improve messaging / I'm not sure.

**Form headline:** Get Your Free Website Review

**Form supporting copy:** We'll look at your website and point out the biggest opportunities to improve clarity, conversion and the path from visitor to enquiry.

**Form CTA:** Request Review

**Confirmation message:**

> Thanks, your website review request has been received. We'll review your website and get back to you within 48 hours with our initial findings.

## 8 — Tracking

Same funnel as the original brief (§21), kept as-is: Spend → Impressions → CTR → Clicks → Form opens → Leads → CPL → Qualified leads → Qualified CPL → Contacted → Conversations → Opportunities → Projects won → Revenue. LinkedIn Campaign Manager for platform-side metrics (CPL, form fill rate, audience performance); everything from "Qualified leads" onward lives in `content/ads/linkedin-website-review/qualified-lead-tracker.xlsx`, built 2026-09-05 (module 3, see §10), since LinkedIn has no visibility into what happens after the form submit.

## 9 — Optimisation rules

Kept from the original brief (§22) unchanged: no wholesale changes before 48 hours, diagnose weak CTR via creative/audience/offer, diagnose weak form-completion via offer/friction/trust/CTA, narrow targeting or strengthen qualification if leads are cheap but low quality, judge expensive-but-excellent leads on cost per qualified opportunity rather than killing the campaign, and favour a clear winner gradually rather than cutting the loser outright. Applies now to two creatives (A vs. D) instead of four.
