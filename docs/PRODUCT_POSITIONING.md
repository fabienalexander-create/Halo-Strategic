## Why Halo's products differ from traditional agencies

Most agencies structure their offerings around what they sell: a website package, an SEO retainer, a paid media service, a content subscription. The service catalogue *is* the business. A prospective client chooses from a menu, and the choice they make is shaped by what the agency happens to offer, not necessarily by what the business actually needs.

Halo's products are structured around a different question: where is this client in their journey toward understanding and fixing their own commercial system? Four stages, not four service categories: Clarity, Strategy, Transformation, Partnership. A client moves through them in the order their own business's condition dictates, they don't pick from a list of things Halo happens to be able to build. Most clients should not be expected to progress through all four, and the site says so directly (the Governing Sentence in `docs/PRODUCT_SYSTEM.md`), which is itself a meaningful point of difference: a traditional agency's positioning implies every client eventually needs everything on the menu.

The clearest evidence of the difference: Commercial Transformation could be delivered by a traditional agency under names like "CRM implementation," "sales enablement," or "marketing automation." What makes it a Halo product instead of an agency service isn't the deliverable, it's that it can't be sold without a preceding Audit naming the specific constraint it addresses. A traditional agency sells CRM implementation to any client willing to buy it. Halo sells a Commercial Transformation project only to a client whose Audit has already identified the constraint it's scoped to fix. Same possible deliverable, structurally different product, because the entry criteria are different.

## How every product reflects the Halo philosophy

Each stage maps onto a chapter of the Philosophy, because the products were built by asking what a business genuinely needs at each point the Philosophy already describes:

- **Clarity** (Health Check, Diagnostic) is Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 made transactable. Chapter 1: "we believe every business deserves a clear diagnosis before making another investment." Chapter 2: diagnosis has to come before prescription, or the diagnosis is just whatever the seller happens to sell. The Diagnostic's own framing, "no promise to fix anything, the promise is clarity", is Chapter 2's argument against manufactured confidence, stated as a product description.
- **Strategy** (Audit) is Chapter 3's "find the constraint, understand why it exists" turned into a single, priced, flagship engagement, deliberately deeper than Clarity because a high-level diagnostic and a full systems investigation are different depths of the same First Law, not different products in kind.
- **Transformation** is Chapter 5's "our loyalty isn't to a service, it's to the diagnosis," structurally enforced: it requires a named constraint from a completed Audit, and is deliberately not named "Implementation," which would suggest a mechanism (software, a system) rather than an outcome (the business working better). The renaming itself is a positioning decision: "that one word elevates the offering" (Fabien, 2026-07-27).
- **Partnership** is Chapter 5's "the best outcome is that, after working together, they need us less than they did when we first met," which sounds like an argument against a retainer product. The Governing Sentence resolves that tension directly rather than leaving it implicit: Strategic Partnership is explicitly framed as something "some clients choose," not the assumed endpoint of every engagement, consistent with Chapter 6's refusal to create dependency.

## How the product journey reinforces "Diagnosis before Prescription"

The journey map isn't just a sales funnel with a fixed order, it's a structural enforcement of Halo's First Law. Two mechanisms do the enforcing:

1. **Entry criteria as gates, not suggestions.** Every product from Strategy onward lists a prior stage's output as its entry criterion. A client cannot buy a Commercial Transformation project without a completed Audit naming the constraint it's scoped to fix. This is what stops the ecosystem from quietly becoming a menu: the gates are what make it a journey.
2. **The Governing Sentence, stated on the page itself.** Rather than relying on entry criteria alone to imply that not every client needs every stage, `/product-journey` now says so explicitly: "Not every client needs a Commercial Audit. Not every Commercial Audit leads to a Transformation project. And not every Transformation requires an ongoing Strategic Partnership." This directly resolves what was previously this document's flagged weak point (the old Partnership stage's soft, unstated entry gate), by naming the discipline outright instead of leaving it implicit in the product structure alone.
