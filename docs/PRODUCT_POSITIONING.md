## Why Halo's products differ from traditional agencies

Most agencies structure their offerings around what they sell: a website package, an SEO retainer, a paid media service, a content subscription. The service catalogue *is* the business. A prospective client chooses from a menu, and the choice they make is shaped by what the agency happens to offer, not necessarily by what the business actually needs.

Halo's products are structured around a different question: where is this client in their journey toward understanding and fixing their own commercial system? Six stages, not six service categories: Discover, Investigate, Design, Implement, Optimise, Partnership. A client moves through them in the order their own business's condition dictates, they don't pick from a list of things Halo happens to be able to build.

The clearest evidence of the difference: three of the four Stage 4 products (Sales System Optimisation, CRM & Automation, Reporting & Commercial Intelligence) could each be delivered by a traditional agency under names like "sales enablement," "CRM implementation," or "analytics dashboards." What makes them Halo products instead of agency services isn't the deliverable, it's that none of them can be sold without a preceding diagnosis naming the specific constraint they address. A traditional agency sells CRM implementation to any client willing to buy it. Halo sells CRM & Automation only to a client whose Audit or Blueprint has already identified CRM/data-trust as the actual constraint. Same deliverable, structurally different product, because the entry criteria are different.

## How every product reflects the Halo philosophy

Each stage maps onto a chapter of the Philosophy, because the products were built by asking what a business genuinely needs at each point the Philosophy already describes:

- **Discover** (Health Check, Diagnostic) is Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 made transactable. Chapter 1: "we believe every business deserves a clear diagnosis before making another investment." Chapter 2: diagnosis has to come before prescription, or the diagnosis is just whatever the seller happens to sell.
- **Investigate** (Audit) is Chapter 3's "find the constraint, understand why it exists" turned into a single, priced product, deliberately separated from Discover as its own stage because a high-level diagnostic and a full systems investigation are different depths of the same First Law, not different products in kind.
- **Design** (Blueprint, Operating System) is Chapter 3's "growth isn't created by activity, it's created by alignment" and Chapter 4's "recover the value you're already creating before creating more," expressed as sequencing and ownership rather than as belief statements.
- **Implement** is Chapter 5's "our loyalty isn't to a service, it's to the diagnosis," structurally enforced: every Stage 4 product requires an entry criterion naming the constraint it addresses.
- **Optimise** is Chapter 3's compounding-friction argument run in reverse: small, regular checks are what prevent that compounding from starting again.
- **Partnership** is Chapter 5's "the best outcome is that, after working together, they need us less than they did when we first met," which sounds like an argument against a retainer product. It isn't a contradiction: Growth Partner and Fractional Growth Strategist exist for businesses that have already reached alignment and want to protect it, not for businesses using the retainer as a substitute for ever finishing the diagnostic work. That distinction is worth stating explicitly in any client-facing copy for this stage, so the retainer doesn't read as the thing Chapter 6 says Halo refuses to do (creating dependency).

## How the product journey reinforces "Diagnosis before Prescription"

The journey map isn't just a sales funnel with a fixed order, it's a structural enforcement of Halo's First Law. Two mechanisms do the enforcing:

1. **Entry criteria as gates, not suggestions.** Every product from Investigate onward lists a prior stage's output as its entry criterion. A client cannot buy Growth Blueprint without a completed Audit, cannot buy a Stage 4 implementation product without a named constraint behind it. This is what stops the ecosystem from quietly becoming a menu: the gates are what make it a journey.
2. **Outcome-naming instead of service-naming at Stage 4.** By naming Implement-stage products after the outcome they produce rather than the mechanism they use, the products themselves carry the diagnosis-first logic in their name. A client asking for "CRM & Automation" without having been through Discover/Investigate first is, definitionally, asking for something outside how the product is defined.

The one place this reinforcement is currently weakest: Stage 6, Partnership. Growth Partner has no stated entry criterion beyond "typically follows the full journey," softer than every other stage's gate. If Partnership is meant to carry the same diagnosis-first discipline as the rest of the ecosystem, its entry criteria may need the same rigor Stage 4 has, a named, ongoing reason for the retainer, re-verified periodically, rather than an open-ended relationship once a client reaches this stage. Worth a decision alongside the Growth Partner / Fractional Growth Strategist distinction flagged in `PRODUCT_SYSTEM.md`.
