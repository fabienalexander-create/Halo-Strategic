# US HVAC LinkedIn Creative Concepts — For Testing, Not Assumed Winners

Status: Built 2026-09-12, grounded in `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md`. None of these are declared "the winner." Per Fabien's explicit framing: the job is to design and run genuinely different concepts intended to **minimise qualified customer acquisition cost**, measured against real spend and real qualified trials sold, not to guess which one is cleverest. Whichever one actually produces the lowest cost-per-paying-HVAC-customer is correct, that's an output of testing, not a property being designed in here.

Audience for all five: US HVAC business owners/decision-makers on LinkedIn (`BRIEF.md` §2), not homeowners.

No creative below promises guaranteed sales, jobs, or specific earnings, per `BRIEF.md` §4 and the non-negotiable against manufactured certainty.

---

## Concept A — "You're not competing with us. You're the only one who gets the call."

**Hook:** Direct attack on the market's dominant complaint (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §2): shared leads sold to 3-4 competitors at once.

**Visual:** Simple split composition, left side shows a homeowner's contact card duplicated four times with four competitor logos/silhouettes crossed through; right side shows one clean, single contact card with Halo's mark. No stock photography.

**Headline:** Stop Splitting Leads With 3 Other Contractors

**Supporting copy:** Most HVAC lead services sell the same homeowner to you and your competitors at the same time. Every opportunity Halo delivers goes to one HVAC company. Yours.

**CTA:** See the $750 Trial

**Format:** Single image, 1:1

**Psychological angle:** Loss aversion + fairness. The complaint is real and evidenced (§2), naming it directly signals Halo understands the actual pain rather than pitching generically.

**Why it's worth testing:** It's the sharpest, most specific claim in the set, high potential to stop the scroll for anyone who's used Angi/HomeAdvisor before, but it's also a promise Halo has to keep in delivery, a false claim here is worse than a generic one.

## Concept B — "Pay for a conversation, not a phone number."

**Hook:** Reframes the unit being sold from "lead" to "qualified, callback-ready appointment" (`BRIEF.md` §4's open question about which framing converts better).

**Visual:** A simple calendar/callback-slot graphic: "Homeowner — Dallas, TX — AC not cooling — Available Tue 2-4 PM — Agreed to speak with a technician." Presented like a clean appointment card, not a lead-list row.

**Headline:** This Isn't a Lead. It's a Booked Conversation.

**Supporting copy:** Halo doesn't sell contact lists. Every opportunity comes with a homeowner who's confirmed they want to talk, and when. You call, you close, nobody else is in the mix.

**CTA:** Get 5 Qualified Appointments — $750

**Format:** Single image, 1:1 and 1.91:1

**Psychological angle:** Specificity builds credibility, a named time window is concrete in a way "qualified lead" isn't, and directly tests the brief's own open question about appointment vs. lead framing.

**Why it's worth testing:** Most likely to filter for buyers who've been burned by dead-number leads specifically (§2), possibly narrower reach than Concept A since it's a more unusual claim to process quickly.

---

## Concept C — "You already know what a dead lead costs. Here's what this costs instead."

**Hook:** Direct cost-transparency play, using real market pricing context (§1) without inventing numbers.

**Visual:** Plain navy background, large typography only, no imagery, contrast-driven: "$150" in one weight, "per qualified opportunity, not per phone number" underneath in a lighter weight.

**Headline:** $150. Per Qualified Opportunity. Not Per Dead Number.

**Supporting copy:** Most HVAC leads cost $45-$300, and a chunk of them are disconnected numbers or homeowners who hired someone else weeks ago. Test 5 real opportunities for $750, see what you actually get.

**CTA:** Start the $750 Trial

**Format:** Single image, 1:1

**Psychological angle:** Price transparency as trust signal, most competitors bury pricing behind a "contact us," stating it plainly is itself a differentiator per `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §5.

**Why it's worth testing:** Removes a whole objection-handling step (price hesitation) before the click, but a stated price this early could also suppress clicks from owners who'd have engaged before seeing a number, worth reading against Concept A/B's click-through rate, not assumed better or worse in advance.

---

## Concept D — "The math, not the pitch."

**Hook:** Uses real job-value context (§3) to frame the trial as a rounding error against potential return, without promising a specific outcome.

**Visual:** Simple two-line comparison, no imagery: "Average HVAC job: $340+" above "Cost per opportunity: $150" with a thin bronze-style rule between them (or whatever the eventual US-HVAC visual system uses, see `BRIEF.md` §6 open item on visual system).

**Headline:** One Job Covers More Than Two Opportunities

**Supporting copy:** The average HVAC ticket is worth $340. An install is worth considerably more. Five qualified opportunities cost $750 total. You do the math on what one closed job is worth to you.

**CTA:** Test 5 Opportunities

**Format:** Single image, 1:1

**Psychological angle:** Reframes $750 from "cost" to "obviously survivable risk" using the reader's own math rather than Halo's claim, avoids the earnings-guarantee trap by never stating what Halo's leads specifically will close at.

**Why it's worth testing:** Most exposed to feeling like every other lead-gen pitch's ROI math (a genre HVAC owners have likely seen many times), needs the flattest, most factual tone in the set to avoid reading as hype.

---

## Concept E — "Stop paying an agency to guess. Start paying for outcomes."

**Hook:** Retainer fatigue, a distinct pain point from the shared-lead complaints (§2), targets owners currently burned by marketing agencies rather than lead brokers specifically.

**Visual:** A crossed-out invoice/retainer graphic ("$X,XXX/month — marketing services") next to a clean, itemised one ("$750 — 5 qualified opportunities").

**Headline:** Stop Paying a Monthly Retainer and Hoping.

**Supporting copy:** No contract. No vague "marketing activity." Pay once, get 5 qualified HVAC opportunities, see exactly what you got before you buy more.

**CTA:** Request the Trial

**Format:** Single image, 1:1

**Psychological angle:** Speaks to a different, adjacent audience segment than A-D, owners who've tried agencies rather than lead brokers, worth testing as a genuinely separate angle rather than a variation on the others.

**Why it's worth testing:** Broadest emotional target (agency fatigue is common across many services, not HVAC-specific), which could mean less HVAC-specific resonance than A-D; also worth checking this doesn't read as ironic given Halo Strategic's own consultancy arm sells retainer-adjacent engagements, a framing risk worth having Fabien sanity-check once §0 of `BRIEF.md` is resolved.

---

## Testing structure (recommendation, not decided)

Same discipline as the LinkedIn website-review campaign (`docs/LINKEDIN_LEAD_GEN_CAMPAIGN.md` §2): running all five concepts on a small initial budget splits the data too thin to read cleanly. Recommend starting with two, **Concept A and Concept C**, the sharpest and most differentiated pair (a specific competitive claim vs. a stark, transparent price claim), holding B, D, and E as a second wave once the first pair produces a real CAC comparison. This is a recommendation for whoever runs the actual test, not a decision made here.
