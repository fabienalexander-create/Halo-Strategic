The Real Problem

Every department can be doing its job well, and the business still underperforms.

Marketing optimises for leads. Sales optimises for conversion. Operations optimises for delivery. Leadership wants profitable growth.

Every one of those can be working exactly as intended, and the business can still underperform, because no one is responsible for how they fit together. Not conflict. Just nobody watching the whole system.

The Wrong Fix

You don't have a lead problem. You have a commercial system you haven't diagnosed yet.

When growth stalls, the instinct is to buy more of whatever sits at the top of the funnel. Sometimes that's the right call. Often it just makes the real problem more expensive, because the constraint sits somewhere the extra volume never reaches.

What usually gets bought
  • More leads
  • More traffic
  • Another channel
  • More content
  • A new website
Where the constraint often sits
  • Positioning. The message is reaching the wrong buyers, or the right buyers can't tell why you're different.
  • Lead quality. Volume that was never going to buy.
  • Qualification. No shared definition of a lead worth working.
  • Follow-up. Leads arriving and not being worked, or worked too late.
  • Sales conversion. Opportunities lost in the conversation, not the campaign.
  • CRM and attribution. Nobody can trace a sale back to where it started.
  • Channel economics. A channel that can't profitably reach more of the market.
  • Creative fatigue. The audience has stopped responding to the same message.
  • Operational leakage. Capacity, pricing or delivery quietly undoing what sales won.

Most of these look like marketing problems from the outside. Very few of them are fixed by more marketing. That's why Halo diagnoses before it prescribes: so the next round of growth investment goes to the constraint, not the symptom.

Pattern Recognition

Diagnosis is faster when you've seen the pattern before.

Built through hands-on commercial growth work.

Fabien's experience spans healthcare, technology, financial services, professional services, consumer businesses and multi-location services, with hands-on work across acquisition, conversion, CRM, tracking, creative and commercial strategy.

Much of that current work happens through his agency role and partner relationships, so the businesses involved are not Halo Strategic clients, and none of them are named here. What it provides isn't a logo wall. It's repetition. Across very different businesses, the same patterns keep turning up:

  • The measurement was the problem, not the channel. Lead-ID capture, CRM integration or conversion tracking had to be fixed before any channel could be judged fairly at all.
  • "More leads" became "better leads." Scaling volume didn't convert, and the real conversation turned to qualification.
  • The channel changed because it was never the constraint. Pivots from one acquisition channel to another, not because the first failed outright, but because the limit sat somewhere else.
  • Pricing logic was negotiated too late. Disagreements about how work should be priced surfaced only once a relationship matured, instead of being settled when the scope was set.
  • Fast starts went quiet. Work onboarded quickly, without an agreed diagnosis behind it, often lost momentum within weeks.

The last one matters most. Speed to start is not the same as speed to results, and it's the clearest argument there is for diagnosing before committing. That's the experience every Commercial Diagnostic draws on. How these patterns typically present, sector by sector, is written up on Selected Engagements.
